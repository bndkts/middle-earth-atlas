# Moria regional map

Clicking Moria on the world map (or choosing it in search) loads `/maps/moria/`.
The Maps menu and the return link load the world map again; its saved viewport
is retained. Existing `/?place=moria` links still show the place panel, which
includes a link to the regional map. Choosing Moria as a directions endpoint
continues to work. Reading-mode spoiler protection is preserved.

The regional map supports pointer drag, two-finger pinch, keyboard panning,
zoom buttons, fit-to-view, an expanded map view, label visibility and nine
addressable landmarks. Selections use URL fragments; browser Back/Forward
restores each entry’s place, centre and zoom. Mobile devices keep a scrollable
description panel and place picker immediately below the map. Without
JavaScript, the illustration, index, all descriptions and return links remain
available. A fresh offline cache includes the regional map and its assets.

## Artwork and maintenance

The original SVG plate is drawn by `scripts/draw-moria.mjs` and generated by
`npm run generate` into `assets/moria-section.svg`. Do not edit the output.
Its 1500 × 1000 viewBox is an illustration coordinate system, with a 3000 × 2000
intrinsic image size. The camera operates on the viewBox coordinates; landmark
anchors in `maps/moria/index.html` are percentages of that same plate. Artwork
metadata is in `window.ATLAS_MAP_IMAGES` in `src/images.js`.

The SVG is self-contained: no raster images, scripts, filters or external fonts.
Tests enforce deterministic generation and a 600 KB ceiling. Gesture frames
update the plate transform, nine screen-space markers, collision-checked names
and zoom controls. Names start hidden on narrow views, with a working toggle. Small numbered
markers sit outside the main scenes and use fine reference lines. Label layout
avoids the main halls, bridge figures, forge and cavern, as well as other labels
and markers. Close views show only the selected place name. Each article's
`data-bounds` frames its illustrated scene independently of `data-pin` and
`data-target`, which place its marker and reference line in illustration units.
Mobile overview uses the plate's aspect ratio; selected places open a closer
view. Layout changes wait for an active pointer gesture to finish.
Viewport dimensions are measured at resize and gesture start, not after writes
inside pointer-move handlers. Pointer cancellation, focus loss and resize clear
active gestures. Keyboard zoom and selection also cancel an unfinished drag.

The page, ES module import and service-worker precache use a matching
`v=moria-8` asset revision. Bump it together when changing the viewer or plate,
so a waiting older service worker cannot combine new HTML with stale assets.

## Passage continuity

Hall passages and mine branches are painted together, one stone layer at a
time, so intersecting corridors share an open interior. Chamber openings are
computed from actual route/room intersections in `scripts/moria-geometry.mjs`.
Wall masks leave those openings clear as the room is drawn, rather than painting
patches over its furnishings. Raised entrances receive stairs to the floor;
vertical walking passages have paired flights and mine shafts use ladders.
Roof entries continue down through the room, and furnishing bays leave the
access flights clear. A rear portal and landing connect the great hall to the
Endless Stair. Tests reject
unattached hall-corridor endpoints, misplaced openings, and halls or mine
networks disconnected from the western entrance. Roughly hatched mining faces
remain deliberate dead ends. These checks establish illustration continuity,
not historical accuracy or a navigable game level. Separate checks cover
stair access, furnishing clearance, the troll's fit beneath its curved vault,
protected illustration areas and desktop/mobile landmark framing. Additional
regressions check the drummer against its curved vault, all four mine carts
against actual passage floors, floor vessels, freestanding braziers, spiral
stair tread marks and corridor-floor landing heights. The full bounds of all twelve
small figures were also checked against the rendered SVG room paths.

The architectural vocabulary distinguishes vaulted galleries, monumental
ribbed halls, curtained dwellings, timber stores, masonry forge hoods and the
single archive vault. Fine stone outlines, uneven ink hatching and broad rock
washes replace the earlier heavy corridor shoulders. The central shaft uses a
lighter, narrower silhouette; the lower cavern has irregular shelves and a
broken shoreline so that the occupied halls lead the composition. Local loading
recesses accommodate the wagons, with short rails, timber supports, lamps and
rubble. Three worked faces expose mineral seams. Sparse uneven masonry joints
break up long passage edges. Stone stair slabs and end landings meet the walking
surfaces rather than the passage centre lines.

## Interpretation and sources

This is an imagined west–east section, with north–south differences projected
onto the same plane. Architecture, tunnel connections and dimensions are
invented. The plate combines periods and depicts the bridge intact. It has no
timeline. Ink miniatures add Orc patrols, an archer, a drummer, a cave troll,
and Gandalf facing the Balrog. These scenes combine story moments; their scale
and placement are illustrative. The Balrog is a dark ink apparition, partly
hidden in smoke, with sparse embers and a thin fire lash. The other figures use
abstract silhouettes, identifying equipment and sparse hatching. Low Orc
helmets, hunched postures, finer weapon contours and restrained edge highlights
keep those figures distinct from Gandalf's hat, pale beard and soft cloak folds. Figures use the main map’s muted rust, olive,
stone and gold palette, with no animation or extra interaction layer.
The architectural details are generated by `scripts/draw-moria-scenes.mjs`;
figure drawings and hatch patterns live in `scripts/draw-moria-figures.mjs`.
The plate does not claim to be an authentic dwarven document or Tolkien map.
These caveats are also available next to the map.

Reading references: *The Fellowship of the Ring*, Book II, “A Journey in the
Dark” and “The Bridge of Khazad-dûm”; *The Two Towers*, Book III, “The White
Rider”. Secondary references used to locate and check those relationships:

- [Seventh Level](https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/s/seventhlevel.html): the
  Twenty-first Hall and Mazarbul above the gates; the limits of a reconstruction.
- [Second Hall](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Second_Hall): the First Deep,
  bridge and eastern exit sequence.
- [Endless Stair](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Endless_Stair): the ascent from
  the deepest dungeon to Durin’s Tower on Celebdil.

Each landmark description links to additional reading. Text and positions
should retain these uncertainty notices when revised.
