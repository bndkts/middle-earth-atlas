01   /   THE WESTERN THRESHOLD

The Doors of Durin

The western entrance opens toward Eregion. Narvi made the doors; Celebrimbor traced their signs in ithildin. Beyond this quiet threshold, the passages climb into the mountain.

02   /   SEVENTH LEVEL

The Twenty-first Hall

A vast hall in the North-end of Moria, with high pillars and shafts that once admitted daylight. The Fellowship rested here before discovering the nearby Chamber of Mazarbul.

Read the lore ↗

03   /   CHAMBER OF RECORDS

Chamber of Mazarbul

A chamber adjoining the Twenty-first Hall, where the dwarves kept their records. The Fellowship found Balin’s tomb and the damaged book recording the last days of his colony.

Read the lore ↗

04   /   FIRST DEEP

The Second Hall

One of the great halls of Old Moria, below the level of the eastern gates. At its eastern end, the narrow bridge crosses a deep chasm; beyond it, stairs lead toward the First Hall and the way out.

Read the lore ↗

05   /   THE EASTERN CHASM

The Bridge of Khazad-dûm

A slender span without a parapet, made to force an enemy to cross in single file. Here Gandalf confronted the Balrog. This plate imagines the bridge intact, before its destruction.

Read the lore ↗

06   /   THE GREAT GATES

The East-gate

Moria’s older entrance faces Dimrill Dale. The First Hall lies inside these gates; outside, the road descends toward the valley and the Mirrormere.

Read the lore ↗

07   /   THE WORKINGS

The Mithril Mines

The wealth of Khazad-dûm lay in mithril, the precious silver-steel mined beneath the mountains. These branching galleries evoke the dwarves’ craft; their arrangement here is invented.

Read the lore ↗

08   /   FROM DEEP TO SUMMIT

The Endless Stair

A winding stair led from Moria’s deepest dungeon to Durin’s Tower on Celebdil. Its full course is not mapped in the text. Here it is drawn as a continuous thread through the mountain.

Read the lore ↗

09   /   BELOW THE KNOWN HALLS

The Deep Places

Far below the bridge lay cold water and the foundations of the mountains. The caverns and pools on this plate are an imaginative evocation of those depths, not a documented survey.

About this drawing & sources

An imagined dwarven survey, with later annotations. Named landmarks come from Tolkien; their positions, connecting passages, architecture and relative scale are reconstructed. This is a west–east cutaway, not a floor plan. Places on different north–south axes are projected onto one section.

The plate combines features from different periods and includes story spoilers. It does not follow the world map’s timeline.

Reading references: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Fellowship of the Ring, Book II, “A Journey in the Dark” and “The Bridge of Khazad-dûm”; The Two Towers, Book III, “The White Rider”. See also the Encyclopedia of Arda on the Seventh Level.
